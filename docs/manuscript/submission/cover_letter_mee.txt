Cover letter: Methods in Ecology and Evolution
=============================================

Dear Editors,

Please find attached our manuscript, "When does sampling uncertainty matter in matrix population models? Evidence from published projection matrices," submitted for consideration as a Research Article in Methods in Ecology and Evolution.

Demographic databases such as COMPADRE and COMADRE have transformed comparative ecology. Yet, the matrix entries they compile are almost never accompanied by the sample-size information needed to quantify estimation uncertainty. The standard practice — treating published matrix entries as fixed values — is widely acknowledged as a limitation, but it has remained unclear when it actually matters. We provide an empirical answer.

Using 695 matrix population models from 31 studies, we show that the consequences of ignoring sampling uncertainty are strongly trait-dependent. For highly integrated metrics such as population growth rate, sampling uncertainty accounts for a negligible fraction of observed variation, and inference is largely unaffected. For mortality-trajectory traits — particularly the shape of the mortality distribution — sampling uncertainty accounts for the majority of apparent among-population variation, and point estimates are often systematically displaced from their sampling distributions by boundary survival estimates. Counterintuitively, incorporating sampling uncertainty into our comparative analysis produced narrower, better-calibrated posteriors, not wider ones, because boundary estimates had artificially inflated the spread of the point estimates.

We translate these findings into a practical diagnostic framework — including heuristic thresholds for flagging fragile analyses — and provide a reproducible workflow that researchers can apply to their own compiled-database analyses. We also identify improved reporting practices for primary MPM studies that would make uncertainty reconstruction routine rather than exceptional.
We believe this work is well-suited to Methods in Ecology and Evolution: it addresses a pervasive methodological gap, delivers actionable guidance for a large and active user community, and includes fully reproducible data and code.

This manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission.
